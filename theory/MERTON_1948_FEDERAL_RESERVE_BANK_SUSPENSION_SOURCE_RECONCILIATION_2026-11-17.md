## Independently surfaced scope conflict
The December 1937 Bulletin reports 5,712 suspensions in 1921–1929 (nine years), average 634.67, matching Merton's approximately 635 if the '1920s' means 1921–1929 rather than calendar 1920–1929.
It reports 9,096 suspensions in 1930–1933 (four years), average 2,274, close to but not identical with Merton's 2,276. The 1937 Annual Report Table 76 reports 1930=1352, 1931=2294, 1932=1456, 1933=4004, totaling 9106, mean 2276.5. Contemporary official revisions / differing suspension definitions are a plausible explanation of the discrepancy; no reconciliation has been established. Do not label one version erroneous.
The December 1937 Bulletin reports 135 suspensions in 1934–1936, average 45, with individual years 57, 34, 44. Merton reports 28 per year for a twelve-year post-legislation window. These are different windows and 45 cannot refute a twelve-year mean. A twelve-year period after the 1934 insurance start might extend beyond the 1937 bulletin. No verified full matching twelve-year series or precise endpoints have been established, so 28 remains unreconstructed.
A February 1943 Bulletin specifies that its suspension figures concern licensed banks, excluding nonlicensed banks placed into liquidation or receivership after the banking holiday. The official source definition explicitly changes the risk of comparing pre- and post-1933 counts without a scope audit.

## Scientific interpretation
These are contemporaneous official descriptive records, stronger for establishing period counts than Merton's unsupported quoted means, but neither alone identifies a causal FDIC effect. The post-1933 change is confounded by bank holiday, licensing, regulation, monetary recovery, population of operating banks, and definition changes. A historical counterfactual cannot be inferred from the observed period averages.

## 2026-11-17 follow-up: the twelve-year mean reconstructed from 1945–46 Federal Reserve originals

New primary sources inspected via FRASER searchable historical tables:
* Federal Reserve Bulletin January 1945, BANK SUSPENSIONS table (1934–1944 annual figures): https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/FRB/1940s/frb_011945.pdf
* Federal Reserve Bulletin July 1946, BANK SUSPENSIONS table (1934–39 aggregate and 1940–45 annual figures), printed p. 771: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/FRB/1940s/frb_071946.pdf

The 1945 Bulletin reports annual counts 1934=57, 1935=34, 1936=44, 1937=59, 1938=55, 1939=42, 1940=22, 1941=8, 1942=9, 1943=4, 1944=1. The July 1946 Bulletin supplies 1945=0, and aggregates 1934–39=291, independently consistent with 57+34+44+59+55+42=291. Hence 1934–1945 inclusive is exactly twelve years, with 291+22+8+9+4+1+0=335 suspensions, and 335/12=27.9166667 per year, rounding to 28. This reconstructs the numerical value and a plausible twelve-year window quoted by Merton (1948, p. 209). It does not prove Merton consulted these specific tables or uniquely establish his source.

The July 1946 table defines suspensions as temporary or permanent closings due to financial difficulty, explicitly excluding closings with deposit liabilities assumed by another bank at closure, sometimes with FDIC assistance. Therefore the series is not a census of every bank exit or every FDIC intervention. Both official tables have been read as searchable transcriptions; visual scan-by-scan collation is still pending. 1930–33 official cross-version discrepancy remains open. Numerical reconciliation is descriptive only, and does not identify the causal effect of FDIC insurance, bank licensing, wartime conditions, or other institutional changes. Independent arithmetic: 1350+2293+1453+4000=9096; 1352+2294+1456+4004=9106; differences 2+1+3+4=10. The Annual Report four-year average is 2276.5, while the Bulletin average is 2274. Thus the two official published tables disagree in *each* of the four years, rather than solely in 1933. This closes the allocation question and establishes the published-year discrepancy, but does not identify which individual banks or revisions account for it.

The December Bulletin Table 1 excludes mutual savings banks; the 1937 Annual Report Table 76 has a shorter header definition. The September Bulletin discusses special holiday exemptions, unlicensed banks and reporting treatment in 1933. These facts do not establish a causal explanation for the four-year differences. A later revision, bank-class inclusion difference or extraction issue remains possible until primary printed scans and source footnotes are collated. Do not label either series erroneous or equate the ten-bank difference with a single named bank category.

Implication for Merton (1948, p.209): a quoted annual 2,276 is close to the Annual Report's 2,276.5 and further from the December Bulletin's 2,274.
