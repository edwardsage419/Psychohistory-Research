## Original claims in inspected pages
1. pp. 297–298 distinguish internal validity (effect of X in the specific experiment) from external validity (generalization to other people, settings and variables). One-group pre/post differences can arise from history, maturation, testing, instrument decay, regression, and selection/attrition in alternative designs.
2. pp. 298–299 distinguish reactive measurement: testing or observation can alter the phenomenon subsequently measured. Thus measurement is sometimes an intervention in its own right.
3. pp. 301–303 explain a stronger limit: even a randomized pretest/posttest controlled experiment can identify an effect in a pretested population while lacking a warranted transfer to unpretested people. The pretest may interact with X. Campbell cites the NORC Cincinnati UN publicity campaign and prior Army and school examples as reported historical studies, not experiments independently reproduced in this note.
4. p. 303 proposes the Solomon four-group design, with randomized pretested treated/untreated and unpretested treated/untreated groups. Comparing the four posttest means separates treatment, pretest, and interaction contrasts under appropriate randomization and measurement assumptions. Campbell explicitly cautions against fabricating pretest scores for unpretested groups.

## Independent constructive identification example
Let P indicate whether a pretest occurred, T randomized intervention, and posttest outcome Y = 2P*T, with zero intercept and no noise. For P=1, treated minus untreated equals 2; for P=0, the same contrast equals 0. A perfectly randomized study restricted to P=1 identifies a treatment effect of 2 for that design yet cannot identify the effect among P=0 subjects from those data alone. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a claim about any human population. In the four-group design, the contrast [(Y11-Y10)-(Y01-Y00)] identifies the pretest-by-treatment interaction on the additive outcome scale, conditional on randomization and consistency; alternative scales need separate analysis.

## Competition, scope, measurement and causal limits
A pre/post change may instead reflect history, regression to mean, maturation, instrument drift, selective dropout, or changes in the construct. Even randomized treatment does not guarantee transport to other measurement regimes or cultures. In individual repeated self-report, repeated questioning may itself change salience, preferences or subsequent action; any proposed future N-of-1 study must log timing, prompts, exposure and missingness, and specify a comparison capable of falsifying a measurement-reactivity claim. A Solomon design in a single person across time also requires strong carryover and temporal stability assumptions; four parallel independent randomized groups cannot be silently replaced by four sequential periods.
