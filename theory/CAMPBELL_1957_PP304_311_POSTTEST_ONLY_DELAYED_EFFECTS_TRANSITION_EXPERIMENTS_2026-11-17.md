## New primary-source claims beyond earlier pp.297–303
1. Printed p.304, Design 6: randomized posttest-only groups can identify an experimental effect without a potentially sensitizing pretest. Campbell argues Design 6 often preferable to the pretest/posttest Design 4 when pretesting reactivity is a concern, while Solomon Design 5 also measures testing interactions. Random allocation A is a particular time point; attrition after A and noncontemporaneous measurement can invalidate the intended comparison.
2. Printed pp.306–307, multiple X and delayed testing: a nominal no-treatment control receives other experiences Xc. Repeated different treatments in the same participants introduce order, learning, and carryover interactions; the isolated-X effect is not automatically recovered by counterbalancing. For delayed effects with reactive outcomes, Campbell calls for new randomized treated and control groups at each measurement horizon, otherwise time-varying history, maturation, instrumentation, regression and attrition remain confounded.
3. Printed pp.307–309, external validity: eligibility, contact, cooperation, dropout and experimental awareness jointly select the observed population. Campbell gives illustrative noncontact and experimental-selection figures; these are author's period-specific arguments, not validated population rates for 2026. Using intact groups may reduce disruption while introducing cluster-level assignment and inference constraints.
4. Printed pp.309–310, representative stimuli and outcomes: even when one treatment X works, a single realization bundles theory-relevant and incidental cues. Campbell proposes varying X and measurement O across implementations; placebo-style controls separate components of the treatment. His 'transition experiment' combines a segment of close replication with variations of X and O to probe transport. This is a distinct methodological issue from pretest reactivity in the previous note.
5. Printed pp.310–311: author explicitly prioritizes internal validity when a trade-off is unavoidable. This is a normative research-design recommendation, not a mathematical proof of universally optimal design.

## Independent constructive identification limit
Consider one person measured at t1 and t2 after exposure. Observed outcomes (1,3) can arise under model A: underlying untreated state remains 1 and a delayed intervention adds 2 at t2; or model B: intervention has zero effect and secular history raises untreated state from 1 to 3. Both fit exactly. Randomizing treatment order across periods without stable washout does not resolve carryover automatically. This is a logical counterexample; it is neither behavioral data nor an R-model validation.

A possible future falsification protocol requires randomized independent treatment/control groups assigned to separate follow-up horizons, consistent nonreactive outcome ascertainment, timestamped exposure, prespecified attrition analysis, and competing time-trend models. In N-of-1 work, simultaneous independent groups are unavailable, so counterfactual identification needs additional stationarity/carryover assumptions or alternative external controls. No private data or human experiment used.

## Competition and boundaries
Alternatives to a treatment effect: history, maturation, measurement practice, order/carryover, selective loss, treatment bundle changes, observer reactivity. Even internally randomized findings may not transport across culture, historical period, measurement instrument, stimulus implementation, or participant self-selection. Campbell's examples are reported prior studies, not independently replicated here. Printed pp.305 and 308–310 read as extracted source text; full page-image line-by-line collation and primary replication of cited experiments remain undone.
