## Distinct research increment
French distinguishes contradictions inside a *particular oracle consultation* from inconsistencies across loosely connected representations. In his account, an internally inconsistent pair of chicken outcomes may invalidate a practical verdict, while apparent contradictions among broad beliefs may remain compartmentalized, with interpretive flexibility. Thus “the Azande ignore contradictions” is overgeneralized: one must specify the level, occasion, and practical consequences. This is French's philosophical interpretation of Evans-Pritchard, not an independently measured population frequency.

French's further claim: cultural commitments can be semi-propositional or representational rather than fully specified truth-apt propositions; partial structures and quasi-truth may represent them without forcing a unique completed logical theory. It is a proposed formal interpretation, not proof that Azande actors employ a particular non-classical logic.

Sankey instead distinguishes the local justification of belief by an epistemic norm from objective comparative appraisal of norms. In §§1–3, he describes a criterion problem: any attempted ultimate justification risks regress, circularity or an ungrounded starting point. His stated program seeks comparative assessment by empirical reliability, developed in §§4–6 (NOT READ). This is a competing philosophical question about norm selection, not an ethnographic experiment.

## Identification and possible discriminating evidence
Observable candidates in a future consented, independent study: same-session detection of mutually incompatible verdicts; willingness to repeat a consultation; cross-session memory and integration; explicit predictions with outcomes independently recorded; social sanctions for consulting or disregarding an oracle. Contradiction detection, predictive calibration, pragmatic action and cultural interpretation must be measured separately.

A participant repeating a test after contradiction would be compatible with local error detection under either French's and Sankey's views. It would not identify a partial-structure cognitive representation. Evidence of cross-session inconsistencies alone cannot distinguish lack of information sharing, strategic non-disclosure, social incentives, contextual reinterpretation, or differences in individual beliefs. Norm performance requires preregistered observable outcomes, a non-circular benchmark, and attention to selection and confounding. No such data were collected.
