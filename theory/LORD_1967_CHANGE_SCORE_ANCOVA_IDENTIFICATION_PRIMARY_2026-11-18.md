## Independent analytical result

Let G=1 or 0 denote a pre-existing group; X the baseline, Y the follow-up. Define dX=E[X|G=1]-E[X|G=0], dY analogously. The group difference in mean change is

  delta_change = dY-dX.

Under a common-slope linear conditional mean E[Y|X,G]=a+bX+tau G, the ANCOVA adjusted group contrast is

  tau = dY-b*dX.

Their difference is tau-delta_change=(1-b)*dX. Thus baseline imbalance and b != 1 suffice for disagreement, even when both analyses are arithmetically correct. The conditional contrast requires a shared slope and overlap in baseline values for empirically supported comparisons; extrapolation may otherwise be necessary.

Constructed example (not empirical data): dX=10, dY=10, b=0.5. The mean change contrast is 0, while the conditional contrast is 5. These values are algebraically consistent with a common-slope regression and distinct group intercepts. The contrast is descriptive conditional association. Without an exchangeability/causal design assumption, it does not identify an intervention effect. The same observations can be generated by different unmeasured selection and natural-history processes.

## Theory competition and boundaries

Lord's original critique concerns nonrandomized pre-existing groups and an ambiguous causal interpretation. Difference scores target mean within-group change; ANCOVA targets baseline-conditional follow-up differences. Neither alone establishes the counterfactual response to changing group assignment. The modern DAG-based critique of change-score analyses is a candidate competing treatment; only its abstract was read in this run, so its detailed identification conditions remain unverified.

Individual measurement candidate C: for future N-of-1 observational monitoring, baseline adjustment versus change-score estimands must be preregistered and linked to explicit causal assumptions. Recording only two time points and an exposure does not generally identify causal response under time-varying confounding, regression to the mean, measurement error, and history effects. Observable variables: baseline, follow-up, exposure, measurement occasions, confounders and missingness; falsifiable design checks include baseline overlap, slope heterogeneity, and negative-control trends.
