## Freeze project mathematical assumptions, non-original utility and its boundary
Odd-number finite citizens N∈{5,7,9}, strictly nonnegative productivities x_i ∈ {0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1}; all labor n_i∈[0,1]. Flat tax t∈[0,1]. Rebate r(t)= t*(sum_i x_i*n_i(t)/N), identical for ALL residents (including nonvoters), funded solely by tax; no debt, no public goods, no deadweight collection cost. Citizens treat r as given when choosing n_i, consistent with a price-taking atomistic assumption even though finite toy economy: strategically, true finite citizens could internalize own contribution to r; this inconsistency with literal finite strategic game must be explicit. Project utility U_i(c_i,n_i)=c_i−n_i²/2, with c_i=(1−t)x_i n_i + r; THIS is linear in consumption, not the paper's strictly concave u(c,l), and no wealth effects on labor. Precisely n_i(t)=(1−t)x_i, earnings y_i=(1−t)x_i², mean squared productivity m2=(1/N)sum_i x_i²; transfers r=t(1−t)m2. Payoff after individually optimizing labor = (1−t)² x_i²/2 + t(1−t)m2.
IR not applicable; strictly budget balanced for all t. Disutility/real resource welfare total = (N*m2/2)(1−t²), hence redistribution creates strictly positive quadratic resource deadweight for t>0 unless all x zero, in which case tie degeneracy must be recorded.
Voter ideal continuous t_i=0 if q_i=x_i²≥m2; else (m2−q_i)/(2*m2−q_i), with m2>0; if m2=0 everyone indifferent, policy selection returns 0 by fixed lexicographic tie rather than asserting meaningful median selection. All t_i∈[0,1/2]. Single-peaked utility in t for each citizen and ideal ordering inverse in q_i, so under an odd-size electorate with fixed resident economy the median voter ideal is Condorcet against any alternative continuous t (potential indifferences yield weak not strict uniqueness). Majority decisions choose pairwise by strict preference, with a tie reported separately rather than silently imposed.
