## Scientific question and scope

Given all six observable expression probabilities for each artificial gate tuple (A,R,P,M) in {0,1/2,1}^4, corresponding to three known demonstrator consequences C and two phases (spontaneous and fully incentivized), which latent quantities are uniquely determined? All probabilities are model-defined exact rational numbers, not observed behavior. No parameter fitting, human or animal data, external validation, or claim of original Bandura empirical identification.

## Frozen generator and evaluation

For every one of the 81 distinct ordered gate tuples compute L=A*R, V=L*P, S_C=V*M*c_C and I_C=V for C ordered (REWARDED, NO_CONSEQUENCE, PUNISHED), c=(1,1/2,1/4). The six-element observable signature is (S_rewarded,I_rewarded,S_neutral,I_neutral,S_punished,I_punished). Group tuples by exact signature, retaining all 81 original tuple identities. Preserve original R303 source and results unchanged.

Predeclared outputs: 81-row source CSV with four gates, L, V, six probabilities and signature ID; complete equivalence-class JSON with member identities, size, distinct L and V values, minimum and maximum L; exact summary of number of classes, class-size histogram, maximum class size, classes with >1 distinct L, worlds in such classes, classes with >1 distinct V, classes with >1 distinct M, and largest L range. Predeclare a counterexample A=1/2,R=1,P=1,M=1 versus A=1,R=1,P=1/2,M=1; these must share all six observables and have different L. Negative controls: A=0 implies zero output for all consequences and phases; identical tuple must always yield identical signature; every tuple belongs to exactly one class; classes partition all 81. Nonzero V signatures algebraically determine V and M from I_rewarded and S_rewarded, but cannot in general distinguish A,R,P. Zero V signatures provide no identification of M. Report whether all classes have a unique V and whether any class has distinct M.
