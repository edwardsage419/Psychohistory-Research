Paper: Grujić and Lenaerts, *Do people imitate when making decisions? Evidence from a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma experiment*, **Royal Society Open Science** 7:200618, [DOI 10.1098/rsos.200618](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.200618). Full author scientific methods describe human square-lattice IPD using a periodic 4×4 lattice, **16 human players per laboratory session, 50 rounds**, with four direct von Neumann neighbors. Two information conditions differ by whether players can observe other humans' payoff feedback and hence potentially imitate successful decisions. **Independence and deduplication:** This 2020 network multi-neighbor experiment is **not** R106's 2025 newly recruited UK dyadic PD experiment, not R095's 2020 Chapman two-round FOFO, not R102's 2025 six-person climate clubs and not R105's 2024 resource game. However coauthor Tom Lenaerts overlaps R096 Montero-Porras et al. 2022, and both arise in Belgium laboratory ecosystems; distinct DOI/experiment design establishes distinct **published experiment**, *not sufficient by itself to prove no sample participants overlap*. Prior to claiming R107 independent human cohort, inspect study lab, recruitment dates and source pseudonyms/non-overlap methods. Do not count synthetic Axelrod computer tournaments as human evidence.

**Not an exact R106 dyadic replication.** This spatial game has four regular peer neighbors, not one distinct counterpart. More defensible R107 target is individual round-t choice or change and previous **source-verified four neighbors** actions; all predictions must be matched same human and round, with original lattice position adjacency reconstituted from source coordinates and periodic wrap. Source rules require a new protocol before any genuine source cell values are interpreted. Different public payoff information conditions imply true peers may be known only by action, or their payoffs may be visible; do not introduce privileged previous payoff information into a treatment whose participants were not shown it.
