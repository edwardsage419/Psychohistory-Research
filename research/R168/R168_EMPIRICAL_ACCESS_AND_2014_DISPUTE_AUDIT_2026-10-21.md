## Nature 2013 original empirical experiment

Derex, Beugin, Godelle and Raymond (2013), *Experimental evidence for the influence of group size on cultural complexity*, Nature 503:389–391, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12774 (publisher original page https://www.nature.com/articles/nature12774, PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24226775/). The original experiment used 2, 4, 8 and 16-person groups, two virtual cultural skills (arrowhead, fishing net) and 15 construction trials. Figure 1 analyzes group-level cultural task maintenance, and Figures 2 and 3 examine skill performance. Group-level experimental replication counts were 15 for two-player groups and 12 for each other size, totaling 51 groups. The paper's original publisher webpage advertises three downloadable Figure 1, 2 and 3 XLS files and supplementary PDF. These are publisher-provided graph source-data files, NOT automatically person-by-round original records.

## Published 2014 disagreement is integral to this research

Andersson and Read (2014), *Group size and cultural complexity*, Nature 511:E1 DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13411 (publisher original https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13411; original author-university version https://escholarship.org/uc/item/55b8w391), argued that an increase in the **probability at least one member draws a complex artifact** can result from the higher number of draws in a larger group, without improved individual cultural learning. They reported a reanalysis disagreeing with some group-size interpretations.

Derex et al. (2014), *Derex et al. reply*, Nature 511:E2 DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13412, publisher https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13412, defended the experimental evidence and distinguished group-level from person-level analyses. The public response reports an individual complex-task-use group-size linear statistic χ²=2.53, df=1, P=0.11, and quadratic statistic χ²=2.15, df=1, P=0.14; these particular individual-level tests are NOT statistically significant at .05. Neither the critic nor reply should be silently treated as a definitive resolution of all outcomes.

For an unchanged independent per-person probability p of producing a particular cultural skill, the chance a group of n contains at least one producer is exactly `1-(1-p)^n` and **necessarily rises with n for 0<p<1**. This is a sampling/extreme-value null, rather than a claim of per-person cultural improvement. R168 must separately model/measure per-person fidelity, group maximum retention and multi-generation accumulation and must never infer one level from another without data.

## Another available historical replication document remains a protocol only

The Social Sciences Replication Project published a planning report by Teck-Hua Ho and Hang Wu concerning Derex 2013, searchable https://doczz.net/doc/8923791/replication-report---social-sciences-replication-project. That five-page document specifies 65 groups in its first planned sample, a potential extension to 155 groups, and the cultural diversity χ² hypothesis. **Its Results sections contain the literal placeholders "[To be added when replication experiments have been completed.]"**. Thus the examined document provides methodological details and a preregistered plan, **no completed external replication outcomes**. No claim of a successful independent replication can be based on it.
