## Standards and source boundary
W3C Bitstring Status List v1.0 Recommendation (2025-05-15) https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-bitstring-status-list/ specifies status purpose revocation/suspension, validity period and optional ttl refresh hint. Revocation is non-reversible under that status purpose. It does not by itself guarantee an offline verifier has the newest published status. RFC 9162 https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html provides signed tree heads and consistency proof mechanisms in certificate transparency; applying these ideas to personal recovery is a candidate analogy, not a claimed W3C requirement. No cryptographic or real-world implementation was executed.

## Candidate controls and privacy tradeoffs
Require account binding, current issuer authority, verified key rotation lineage, a protected monotonic checkpoint and independently trustworthy time before accepting an offline status. Detect contradictory signed states for the same epoch and hold UNKNOWN. Keep status requests minimally identifying; local verification of a public status list may reduce online verifier disclosure, subject to update freshness. When devices cannot reconcile checkpoints, quarantine high-impact recovery and allow human dispute. Device-held checkpoint can itself roll back with an old backup. Key revocation and compromise cannot be solved by signatures alone without a trusted current authority source.
