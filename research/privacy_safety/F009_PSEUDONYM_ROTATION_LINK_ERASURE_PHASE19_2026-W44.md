Actual local Python standard-library test research/privacy_safety/f009_phase19_pseudonym_rotation_20261030.py returned PASS. Six fictional subjects had epoch-specific HMAC-SHA256 pseudonyms from two different fictional keys. All six identifiers changed and 12 pseudonyms were distinct. A six-entry artificial direct link table was cleared in memory. With appeal status plus relative-day bucket retained, four of six records remained uniquely matchable across epochs by those fields, while two shared one ambiguous signature. Redacting those two fields in the second epoch reduced unique matches under this toy rule to zero. The counts depend on constructed data and attacker knowledge. Hard-coded fictional keys and memory clearing do not demonstrate secure cryptographic erasure, actual storage/backup deletion or real-world anonymity.

Candidate future controls: rotate pseudonyms by purpose and epoch, separate and protect key/link tables, minimise stable quasi-identifiers, document retention and backups, test residual linkability, preserve only narrowly justified pending appeal evidence with expiry and human review. Avoid promising that deleting one key removes all copies or auxiliary linkability. No user sensitive data, live credentials, account recovery, external test or paid service.
