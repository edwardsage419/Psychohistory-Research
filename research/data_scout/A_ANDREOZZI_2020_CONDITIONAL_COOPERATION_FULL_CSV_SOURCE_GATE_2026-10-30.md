## Actual raw row validation
The entire 425701-byte published cleaned CSV was read and parsed in memory, with quote-aware CSV parsing. 1340 records, 67 fields, 134 unique subject_unq IDs, six sessions, ten ordered periods, 67 distinct session/group identifiers. Exactly ten records per participant. Treatments CondInfo 660 rows (66 participants), NoCondInfo 680 rows (68 participants). Six sessions have 22,20,24,22,22,24 subjects respectively. Every (subject_unq,period) key unique; every recorded opponent partner in the same period exists and reciprocally names the subject; group agrees; action/opp_action values agree in both directions. Missing opponent links=0; reciprocity failures=0; cross-action mismatches=0; missing action=0; CSV column-count failures=0. There are 67 dyadic pairs per period. Distinct opponents per subject range 6 to 10, showing repeated exposure and dependence.

Fields include period, subject_unq, session_unq, group, opp_unq, action, opp_action, treatment, conditional choices and beliefs. Actions take numeric values 0,50,100. Source README describes conditional cooperation and transfer tasks, rather than the R073 frozen 100-round binary Prisoner's Dilemma design. The record-level data have been read; this is an E2 *data provenance/measurement inspection* only, with zero new E2 predictive model tests and no E3.

## Replay readiness and gaps
Candidate useful for conditional cooperation, belief dynamics, group/partner turnover and longitudinal individual behavior. A valid split must group by subject and session, with temporal leakage and partner dependence addressed. Exact trial time beyond ordered period, partner reassignment design, original raw z-Tree byte equality, treatment randomization details and preprocessing transformation have not been independently replayed. No original participant records are copied into this research repository. R073 eligibility: NO due to task and horizon mismatch; frozen model and score remain unchanged. Data license requires attribution. No fees or private records.
