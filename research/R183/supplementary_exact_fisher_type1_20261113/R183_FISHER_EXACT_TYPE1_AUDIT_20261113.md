## Specific nonduplicative question

R183 already enumerated 45 artificial two-group count worlds, 4125 source pairs, likelihood separation, heldout Brier/logloss, and the later 45-world sign/contrast-MSE audit. R177 already analyzed marginal means and within-cluster variance. This study investigates a different failure mode: **How often would a nominal 5% two-sided Fisher conditional exact test report a difference when the true group-average success probabilities are exactly equal, if the investigator incorrectly treated correlated events within each group as independent trials?**

The source model contains two independent clusters, one for each `x=0` and `x=1` group. A cluster holds `n=4,8,12` exchangeable events, possibly dependent through an urn/Beta distribution or shared structural inactivity. A conventional 2×2 table treats all `2n` events as independent Bernoulli observations, violating this source model for four of the five mechanisms. This single-cluster-per-group model has **no independent within-arm replication**, so a generally valid cluster-robust effect test cannot be estimated from it. The exercise is a theoretical misspecification stress test.

The frozen R183 false-effect scenario fixes `p0=p1=1/4`. It has 3 group-size choices and 5 source mechanisms, giving 15 distinct null worlds and `5×(25+81+169)=1375` count-pair support points. Their values and weights are entirely specified by the original R177 and R183 frozen research. This is a *postfreeze extension* of those fixed source models; the choice of Fisher's test and α=.05 was made for this new audit after the original R183 work. No original statistical acceptance target is retroactively altered.

## Exact methods and two independent source routes

For an observed `(k0,k1)` with `n` Bernoulli attempts in each group and `s=k0+k1`, the assumed-independence, fixed-margin hypergeometric probability of a candidate count `j` is

`h(j | s,n)=C(n,j) C(n,s-j) / C(2n,s)`.

We use the explicitly defined probability-ordered **two-sided** Fisher p-value `p_F(k0,k1)=Σ_{j: h(j)<=h(k0)} h(j)` and reject on `p_F <= 1/20`. This matches the two-sided convention specified in the English R `stats::fisher.test` manual: https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/fisher.test.html and the Minitab formula note https://support.minitab.com/en-us/minitab/help-and-how-to/statistics/tables/how-to/cross-tabulation-and-chi-square/methods-and-formulas/test-for-2x2-tables/ . Other two-sided conventions exist; their rates are outside this audit.

Each table mass is also independently recomputed by distributing `s` successes across the chosen first group:

`h(j|s,n)=C(s,j) C(2n-s,n-j)/C(2n,n)`.

Every feasible table in n=4/8/12 was checked with exact Python `Fraction` against both formulas. These are two combinatorial derivations of the **same assumed independence reference law**; this check validates arithmetic but cannot make that law appropriate for correlated data.

For the **true source law**, group success counts are independent across groups, with marginal `π_m(k|n,p)`:

* `IID_BIN`: `C(n,k) p^k (1-p)^(n-k)`.
* `CLUSTERED_BB_C2`, `C8`: `C(n,k) (p c)^(↑k) ((1-p)c)^(↑(n-k)) / c^(↑n)`, where `c=2,8` and `x^(↑m)` is the rising factorial.
* `INACTIVE_ZIB_Z1_8`, `Z1_4`: `z 1[k=0]+(1-z) C(n,k) q^k(1-q)^(n-k)`, `q=p/(1-z)`, `z=1/8,1/4`.

Every source marginal was computed twice, once with the closed form above and once with a trial-by-trial **sequential state dynamic program**, in which the BB transition at event `t+1` is `(pc+k)/(c+t)` and ZIB is generated by an independent active-cluster process mixed with an inactive point mass. Both routes match exactly in all 15 worlds. They reproduce the frozen R183 original `n=4` source PMFs and complete-separation probabilities in all five mechanisms, whose exact values were independently read from `research/R183/R183_EXACT_RESULTS.json` (blob `81f8fa790f0dea2715d99e1384ec33a6d2cf7a64`).

The new **true null** rejection rate is the exact finite sum

`P_m(reject | p0=p1=1/4)=Σ_(k0,k1) π_m(k0|n,p) π_m(k1|n,p) 1[p_F(k0,k1)<=1/20]`.

Every probability, null rate, count variance and within-cluster correlation is an exact rational number. Displayed percentages are rounded for readability. No simulation error, bootstrap or inferential standard error applies to this synthetic exhaustive sum.

### Same intracluster correlation, different significance tails

At `p=1/4`, both `BetaBinomial(c=8)` and `ZIB(z=1/4)` have exact ICC **1/9** and therefore identical count means and variances for each fixed n. This follows from the beta-binomial `(c+1)^-1` identity and the ZIB mixture's `z p / ((1-z)(1-p))` ICC formula. Nonetheless, for `n=12`, their Fisher null rejection rates are respectively **10.802110%** and **14.859101%**. Equal first two moments do **not** determine the extreme 2×2-table probabilities. A variance-only correction cannot be presumed to supply exact tail calibration across distinct source distributions.

### Known-full-null-PMF oracle control

As a deliberately idealized control, the code additionally constructs the true null distribution of `|K1-K0|`, calculates its exact discrete survival-tail p-value and tests it at 5%. Each of the 15 model-based oracle rejection rates is <=5%, directly confirming an appropriately calibrated reference distribution can control null rejection in these specified mechanisms. This **oracle knows p=1/4 and the entire data-generating mechanism**. It is not a practical inferential method given only one cluster per group and unknown latent parameters, and it does not demonstrate a realistic alternative test is automatically available.

## Source and interpretation limits

Primary intellectual anchors: Cox (1958) binary sequences, DOI `10.1111/j.2517-6161.1958.tb00292.x`, and Albert & Anderson (1984) logistic parameter existence, DOI `10.1093/biomet/71.1.1`. These sources motivate the existing R183 binary model; they are not claimed to report this project's Fisher null rates. A relevant external, separately empirical illustration of intracluster dependence is Bosen (2024), DOI `10.1121/10.0024633`, which describes beta-binomial modeling of correlated speech-scoring observations; **no data from that paper was used**.

The user's R-series order and frozen R073/PESEN v0.1 protocols are unchanged. Results depend on two independent group units, identical null marginal p, fixed known artificial dependence mechanism, and fully enumerated finite probability support. They do not imply that all logistic analyses in real research are invalid, or that the observed percentage occurs in humans. The immediate scientific implication is an **identifiability and calibration requirement**: when trials are nested within one experimental unit, recognize the unit of independence before evaluating reported p-values; the artificial example provides an exact negative control for science acceptance.
