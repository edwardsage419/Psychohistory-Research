## Original publisher/author studies

* Baronchelli, Felici, Loreto, Caglioti and Steels (2006), [*Sharp transition towards shared vocabularies in multi-agent systems*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/2006/06/P06014), *Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment* P06014, [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0509075) and [City University research copy](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/2672/) were located. This original microscopic naming-game theory concerns self-organized shared vocabularies, peer negotiation and population-level agreement. We will only construct a **finite fixed two-word toy**; we will not reproduce their observed/derived large-network Zipf-like frequency distribution or phase-transition scaling.
* Baronchelli, Dall'Asta, Barrat and Loreto (2006), [*Topology-induced coarsening in language games*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.015102), *Physical Review E* 73, 015102(R), [American Physical Society original](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.015102). The authors distinguish network geometry from individual updates, making a finite contact-graph contrast substantively relevant; their scaling results are not a four-person exact enumeration prediction.
* Nowak, Komarova and Niyogi (2001), [*Evolution of universal grammar*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.291.5501.114), *Science* 291:114–118, original [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11141560/) describes the coherence threshold in grammar-learning dynamics. Different scientific target from negotiated word inventories, so any R170 naming-game output must not be interpreted as grammar emergence or a biological universal grammar model.
* Abrams and Strogatz (2003), [*Modelling the dynamics of language death*](https://doi.org/10.1038/424900a), *Nature* 424:900, [original Springer Nature publisher page](https://www.nature.com/articles/424900a) studies language-population competition fitted to multiple historical language-frequency series. Real Welsh/Scottish Gaelic/Quechua historical observations were not acquired or fitted; the finite naming-game does not provide language-extinction hazard estimates.

## Nonduplication choice

R169 modeled **node opinions and adaptive graph rewiring**. The subsequent R170 design fixes four small graph topologies and models each agent's **internal inventory of potentially two word variants** plus ordered speaker-listener transmission, successful acknowledgment and failure-to-retrieve updating. It contains a distinct internal-state mechanism: an agent can temporarily know both A and B, which R169 binary opinion states cannot represent. Network connections remain fixed; no R169 rewiring repeated as new evidence. 
The original naming-game vocabulary can expand with invented words and uses broader rules and simulations; R170 will deliberately restrict vocabulary to two artificial words, no empty inventories and explicit speaker/hearer rule variants.

Do not claim real language history, modern speech attitudes, linguistic ability, actual communicative complexity, mass grammar drift, or human subject data. No original author R/Matlab simulation or empirical output has been reproduced. E1 exact combinatorial counterexamples only. Maintain R073 and PESEN v0.1 frozen. Complete all R models before taxonomy/fusion.
