# Lord, Ross & Lepper (1979): decision evidence assimilation boundary

Actual research date: 2026-11-21. Track: personal decision theory. Status: candidate research and synthetic acceptance preparation; no empirical implementation validation.

## Source and actual access

## Evidence
Publisher abstract reports 48 undergraduates with opposing preexisting positions about capital punishment. Each encountered two purported studies with opposing implications for deterrence. Participants evaluated congenial evidence more favorably and reported shifts in their respective prior positions. The experimental studies were presented as purported studies; this is a demonstration of evidence appraisal under manipulated information, not evidence that either deterrence claim is true. No individual-life decision effect size established.

## Decision-system gap
A user may ask for evidence supporting a preferred job, location or investment option. Evidence selection and appraisal can become asymmetric. Preserve user autonomy and avoid treating every asymmetric assessment as irrational; studies may truly differ in validity.

## Executable candidate question
'你目前倾向哪个方案？对于支持和反对该方案的证据，你愿意先使用相同的质量检查标准吗？哪些证据会使你重新考虑？'

## Candidate explanation and observable indicators
Record prior preferred option and timestamp; separately store each claim's direction, source, study design, population applicability, measured outcome, methodological quality and evidence date. Ask for explicit counterevidence threshold before displaying outcomes where feasible. Apply the same quality questions to congenial and uncongenial sources. Flag unbalanced search coverage as incomplete rather than calculating a fictitious bias score. Keep value preferences distinct from empirical beliefs.

## Six synthetic acceptance counterexamples (not executed)
1. User prefers A; system retrieves only supportive A evidence and claims comprehensive coverage. Expected: mark coverage incomplete.
2. Two studies have different methodological quality; system forces identical evidential weight merely because they disagree. Expected: distinguish symmetric review standards from equal evidential weights.
3. A source contradicts user's preferred option; system labels user biased solely for questioning its external validity. Expected: assess validity and population before psychological attribution.
4. User changes values after reading evidence; system silently rewrites initial preferences. Expected: preserve dated versions and explicit user confirmation.
5. Opposing evidence uses a different outcome and population; system directly averages contradictory effect sizes. Expected: mark noncomparable.
6. Fictional manipulated study is entered as real evidence of a public-policy effect. Expected: identify experimental stimulus as purported, not actual outcome evidence.

## Assumptions, competing accounts, limits
Hypotheses include motivated reasoning, differing background knowledge, legitimate methodological criticism, source trust, prior plausibility, attention and measurement effects. The original task concerns contested social-policy beliefs among 48 undergraduates. It cannot determine causal effectiveness of symmetric evidence displays for real personal decisions. Research synthesis only. No raw data, reanalysis, program tests, independent replication, or scientific acceptance.
