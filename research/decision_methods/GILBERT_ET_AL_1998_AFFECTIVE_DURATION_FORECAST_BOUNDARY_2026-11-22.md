# Gilbert et al. (1998): negative affect duration forecasts and decision boundaries

Date completed: 2026-11-22 (Asia/Singapore). Track: personal decision theory research. Status: primary abstract verified; full experimental tables and independent acceptance pending.

## Decision gap and scope
For major career, relationship, or relocation decisions, a user may predict that a possible negative outcome will cause prolonged distress. Preserve that forecast as the user's forecast; avoid silently converting it into a numerical correction or overriding a hard constraint. Separate forecasted initial intensity, duration, later adjustment, and non-emotional material consequences.

## Primary source and actual reading
Gilbert, D. T., Pinel, E. C., Wilson, T. D., Blumberg, S. J., & Wheatley, T. P. (1998). "Immune neglect: A source of durability bias in affective forecasting." Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 75(3):617–638. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.75.3.617.
Primary indexed abstract read at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9781405/ ; publisher-origin article copy located at https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/biases/75_J_Personality_Social_Psychology_617_%28Gilbert%29.pdf . This pass verified bibliographic metadata and the abstract; full PDF experimental methods, tables, effect sizes and statistical analysis NOT independently audited. Abstract reports six studies across romantic breakup, tenure denial, election loss, personality feedback, child's death account and prospective employer rejection. The authors interpret the observed durability overpredictions in terms of neglect of psychological adaptation mechanisms. This does not establish a universal correction factor or reliable individual forecast.

Secondary author synthesis for competing mechanism context: Wilson & Gilbert (2005), "Affective Forecasting: Knowing What to Want," Current Directions in Psychological Science 14(3), DOI 10.1111/j.0963-7214.2005.00355.x, publisher abstract https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.0963-7214.2005.00355.x . It also identifies focalism and the failure to anticipate coping; this is a review, not an independent replication of every 1998 result.

## Candidate minimal interaction rule
Give a useful first-pass comparison without requiring a questionnaire. Only if the predicted emotional duration changes the recommendation, ask: “你担心的结果发生后，最难受的是最初几天，还是半年后仍可能持续？你依据什么判断？” Store the user's exact forecast and horizon, explicitly mark evidence unknown, and optionally compare a persistent-impact scenario with a partial-adaptation scenario without assigning fabricated probabilities. Keep economic, physical, relational and irreversible harms as separate dimensions. Never tell the user that they will necessarily adapt.

## Observable indicators and competing explanations
Possible future measurement with explicit consent: prospective forecast at specified horizon and later voluntary experience rating at same horizon, with attrition and response shift noted. No private observations in public repository. Alternatives: actual chronic consequences, persistent health or financial loss, changing external conditions, selective follow-up, individual differences, focal attention, recall bias and genuine persistent preferences. A group-level laboratory effect is insufficient to diagnose an individual or justify changing their utility weights.

## Six synthetic acceptance counterexamples (not executed)
1. A user predicts sadness for six months; the system silently substitutes two weeks: FAIL, forecast version and ownership violated.
2. A job-loss scenario includes loss of housing; system discounts material harm because people adapt emotionally: FAIL.
3. A user identifies an irreversible medical risk; the system treats possible adaptation as permission to cross a hard constraint: FAIL.
4. The user says “I will be devastated” without a horizon; the system fabricates a six-month quantified trajectory: FAIL.
5. The system reports a follow-up only from respondents who recovered and treats missing severe cases as proof of recovery: FAIL.
6. At later review, the user reports lower distress; the system labels the original decision irrational based solely on the realized outcome: FAIL, counterfactual remains unobserved.

## Handoff and acceptance
Model track: test conditional follow-up trigger, preserve original forecast and horizon, compare two qualitative scenarios with no unsupported probability. Data track: assess prospective versus retrospective ratings, consent, attrition, and construct comparability. Independent acceptance track: hold out the six examples, compare against no-question baseline and test unauthorized preference edits and hard constraints. Research track: full-text study-by-study audit and search for counterevidence before any numerical calibration.
