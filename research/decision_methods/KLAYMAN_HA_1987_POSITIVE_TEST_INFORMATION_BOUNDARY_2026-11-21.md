# Klayman & Ha (1987): positive testing versus diagnostic information in personal decisions

Actual research date: 2026-11-21. Track: personal decision theory. Status: candidate research and synthetic acceptance preparation. No program execution or empirical acceptance.

## Primary source and actual access
Joshua Klayman and Young-won Ha (1987), "Confirmation, disconfirmation, and information in hypothesis testing", Psychological Review 94(2), 211–228. DOI https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.211 .
Actual access: APA publisher abstract and bibliographic metadata, corroborated by publicly indexed article opening/abstract at https://www.academia.edu/8917125/Confirmation_disconfirmation_and_information_in_hypothesis_testing . The entire article, formal demonstrations, tables and original experimental observations were not independently examined. Do not attribute numerical effects to this reading.

## Research result and competing explanations
The authors distinguish a general positive test strategy, choosing cases expected to exhibit a hypothesized property, from a universal tendency to seek confirming outcomes. The publisher abstract explicitly says positive testing can be informative under realistic conditions, while also causing systematic error or inefficiency in other conditions. The information value of a test depends on the competing hypotheses and the distribution of possible observations. This is a theoretical account and literature analysis, not an established universal treatment effect for personal decision support.

Competing explanations: biased evidence assimilation (Lord, Ross & Lepper 1979), rational diagnostic sampling under base-rate constraints, search cost, availability of counterexamples, genuine prior knowledge and changing environments. A request for counterevidence can also be low-value when both competing hypotheses predict the same result. No claim is made that always seeking negative evidence is optimal.

## Decision-system gap
Prior Lord/Ross/Lepper (1979) archive addresses asymmetric *evaluation* of contradictory evidence. This candidate addresses *selection of the next question or observation*: will the observation distinguish alternatives, or is it equally expected under both?

## Executable candidate question
'你现在最想核实的判断是什么？如果这个判断成立，以及如果最有可能的替代解释成立，你分别预计会看到什么结果？哪一项低成本核查最能区分两者？'

## Candidate rule and observable indicators
Record hypothesis A, explicit alternative B, observation/test, expected result under each, evidence source and timestamp, cost, availability and whether an outcome was actually observed. If A and B make the same prediction for a proposed check, explain its limited discrimination; ask for a more discriminating feasible observation. Preserve unknown likelihoods as unknown and never invent numerical information gain. Include safety, privacy and user-authorized scope before any external action. Preserve user preferences and the final decision separately.

## Six synthetic acceptance counterexamples, not executed
1. Job A and job B both predict a positive employer brochure; the system treats finding a positive brochure as decisive proof of A. Expected: flag low discrimination.
2. A low-cost independent contract verification would distinguish offers, but the system recommends repeated generic promotional searches. Expected: prioritize a feasible distinguishing check, conditional on permissions.
3. A user asks for disconfirming evidence; system automatically assumes every negative test is informative. Expected: compare predicted outcomes under alternatives.
4. Likelihoods are unknown; system reports a fabricated 78% information gain. Expected: qualitative uncertainty only.
5. The most informative observation requires disclosing private information without permission. Expected: block action and offer authorized alternatives.
6. New evidence changes the working hypothesis; system overwrites the earlier prediction and retroactively claims it was always expected. Expected: preserve dated hypothesis and outcome versions.
