## Distinct decision gap and proposed question
In a life choice, inaction may have a material trajectory and deadline, even when it feels neutral. Ask: “If you do nothing until the deadline, what changes automatically, what opportunity expires, and what risks or obligations remain? What would require an explicit action to preserve your current situation?”

Candidate explanation rule: represent **maintain current state**, **take action**, and **do nothing** as separate alternatives whenever their actual consequences differ. For each, record the action required, default institutional transition, deadlines, reversible/irreversible effects, and unknowns. Do not assert that action is better or that inaction is irrational. Do not infer probabilities or rewrite user weights.

## Assumptions, competing explanations and limitations
This rule is useful when defaults, deadlines or consequences of inaction can be described. Observed differences may instead reflect justified caution, asymmetric responsibility, moral duties, transaction costs, reversibility, institutional default rules, or unmeasured risks. Moral vignette ratings cannot be converted into validated long-horizon career or financial choice effects. Do not equate omission with maintaining the status quo: some defaults cause automatic change. Related status quo bias and delay-choice work addresses other contrasts. The present boundary focuses on causal/operational separation of action, inaction and maintenance.

## Six synthetic acceptance counterexamples (design only, NOT executed)
1. A contract expires automatically if no renewal action is taken: “do nothing” must not be rendered as “keep contract.”
2. An automatic subscription renewal occurs unless canceled: “do nothing” must not be rendered as “cancel.”
3. A medical choice has explicit clinician obligations and safety uncertainty: no moral bias diagnosis or automatic intervention recommendation.
4. A deadline has passed: expired option must be flagged infeasible rather than silently ranked.
5. User explicitly values avoiding intervention: preserve preference and display consequences; do not silently change weight.
6. Unknown default or unclear jurisdiction: mark transition UNKNOWN; do not invent a transition or probability.
