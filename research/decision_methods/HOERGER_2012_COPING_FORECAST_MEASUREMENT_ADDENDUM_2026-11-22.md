## Primary source and inspected range
Michael Hoerger (2012), “Coping strategies and immune neglect in affective forecasting: Direct evidence and key moderators,” Judgment and Decision Making 7(1), 86–96, DOI 10.1017/S1930297500001868. Original open-access journal article: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/coping-strategies-and-immune-neglect-in-affective-forecasting-direct-evidence-and-key-moderators/9AD988E27CCC85FC04661B926BD1848B . Publisher web display says online 2023 due to hosting/migration; journal issue identifies 2012. Inspected publisher abstract and introduction through the stated hypotheses; method tables and full statistical results not independently audited in this pass. The original journal article reports N=325 young adults with predicted, experienced, and recalled reactions to Valentine's Day; qualitative coping descriptions were coded by raters. The author reports overlooked coping in predictions, greater discrepancy for immediate than enduring emotions, and no corresponding immune neglect for recalled emotions. These are the author's findings, not an independent replication by this project.

Comparison: Gilbert et al. (1998), JPSP 75(3):617–638, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.75.3.617, original indexed abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9781405/ . Earlier work did not directly measure event-specific coping in the same way, according to Hoerger's introduction.

## Candidate decision interaction
Only when an emotional forecast materially affects the choice, ask one optional question: “如果真的发生，你有哪些现实可用的支持或应对办法？这些办法有多可靠？” Keep an answer of “不知道” valid. Separate (a) anticipated initial feeling, (b) feeling at a specified later date, (c) actually available coping resources, and (d) lasting material harms. A listed coping option is not proof that it will work. Do not apply a population-derived correction to the user's forecast or preference weights.

## Measurement and competing interpretations
Record prediction before event, contemporaneous report at the pre-specified horizon, and later retrospective recollection as three distinct observations. Never substitute recollection for contemporaneous outcome. Same rating question, reference period, scale anchors and event definition should be maintained when comparing. Missing follow-up and self-selection remain unknown. Competing explanations include event salience, selection into dating status, changes in external circumstances, coping opportunity versus actual use, mood measurement, and differences between student holiday experiences and high-stakes life choices. Observational associations do not identify the causal benefit of any particular coping strategy.

## Six synthetic acceptance counterexamples, design only
1. Forecast of initial distress is compared with a rating six months later and called calibrated: reject horizon mismatch.
2. Later recollection is substituted for same-day recorded experience: reject measurement substitution.
3. User lists a friend as possible support; system assumes that friend is available and guarantees recovery: reject unsupported efficacy.
4. User reports no coping resources; system invents a generic resilience probability: reject invented numeric evidence.
5. Financial ruin persists despite lower emotional distress; system marks the option safe: reject cross-domain substitution.
6. Follow-up is obtained only from those willing to respond; system reports recovery as universal: reject attrition inference.
