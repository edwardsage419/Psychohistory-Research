This original paper models stochastic neighboring territorial state invasions, **not a fitted model of citizens' real elections**. Holley & Liggett (1975), *Ergodic theorems for weakly interacting infinite systems and the voter model*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176996306. Sood, Antal & Redner (2008), *Voter models on heterogeneous networks*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.77.041121. The finite one-step four-node calculation here is a project-selected exact benchmark, not a demonstration that real-world political decisions follow this rule. Four fictional nodes with all16 binary initial configurations. Three undirected graph topologies: PATH4 (degree sequence1,2,2,1;6 possible directed updating pairs), CYCLE4 (all degree2;8 pairs), COMPLETE4 (all degree3;12 pairs). In one step select target site uniformly1/4, then one graph neighbor uniformly by target's degree, copy that neighbor's previous bit and leave all others unchanged. There are 16×3=**48 artificial worlds** with **16×(6+8+12)=416 full originally generated directed event source rows**, probability per row1/(4degree(target)), allowing same-opinion updates that leave the entire configuration unchanged. No user or person actually changes a political vote.

Finite graph stochastic copying has a sharp distinction between simple fraction U(x)=Σx_i/4 and degree-weighted fraction W_G(x)=Σdegree_i*x_i/Σdegree_i. Under site-selected uniform target and neighbor copying on an undirected graph, conditional expectation **E[W_G(x_next)|x]=W_G(x)** because the sum over directed edges of x_neighbor−x_target cancels pairwise. On the two regular graphs W=U so plain density is also a one-step martingale. On irregular PATH4, **plain density need not be a martingale**.

### Frozen one-step counterexample

With original 4-bit configuration (1,0,0,0):
- PATH4 initial plain density **1/4**; exact next expected plain density **7/32**. The endpoint0 loses its opinion1 with probability1/4 while adjacent degree2 node1 can adopt1 with probability1/8, yielding expected count7/8 divided by4=7/32. Initial **degree-weighted** density **1/6**, and exact expected next also **1/6**.
- CYCLE4 and COMPLETE4 both regular; for the same initial configuration next expected plain density remains **1/4**, matching initial.
- Both consensus all-zero and all-one states are absorbing on all three graphs. Every update changes at most one target bit, and every row probability sums to1 by world. This is exact single-step expectation math, **not** proof of eventual consensus rate, long-run fixation, uncertainty around elections, empirical opinion network dynamics or historical changes.
