## Original English sources and empirical distinction

W. G. Runciman (1966), *Relative Deprivation and Social Justice: A Study of Attitudes to Social Inequality in Twentieth-Century England*, Routledge and Kegan Paul. The book reports a national sample survey from 1962 and distinguishes perceived relative standing and reference groups from objective social inequality. Publisher source: https://www.routledge.com/Relative-Deprivation-and-Social-Justice-A-Study-of-Attitudes-to-Social/Runciman/p/book/9781042033027 .

Faye J. Crosby (1976), *A Model of Egoistical Relative Deprivation*, *Psychological Review* 83(2):85–113, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.83.2.85. The account discusses a conjunction of comparison with similar others, wanting a good, perceived entitlement, perceived feasibility and external attribution of lacking the good. Its theory is richer than a simple income gap. Stouffer et al. (1949), *The American Soldier: Adjustment During Army Life*, provides earlier empirical motivation, but no source data has been read in this project.

The original 1962 survey and 1976 source are literature, not project-recovered E2 datasets. Historical claims cannot be inferred from the books' publication or a toy model.
